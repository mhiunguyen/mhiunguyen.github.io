EcoBack — From a First Failure to National First Prize

Not every award-winning project reaches the finish line. EcoBack was the experience that taught me a good idea is not always a deployable real-world solution.

1ST PRIZE · NATIONAL SV_STARTUP VIII Full-Stack Web Prototype · Business Model & Strategy
Live Demo -> University News ->
01

Starting from a Failure

My team started at the Digital Business Contest organized by VECOM, under the theme of green e-commerce. Our initial idea was to build a platform combining delivery services with a cashback mechanism through post-use packaging collection.

However, we failed to advance, falling 4 points short of the threshold. One reason was that AI-generated images in our slides were not thoroughly reviewed, resulting in a zero score for that section. That was my very first lesson: never assume AI output is accurate or ready to use without verification.

02

The Journey & Pivots

After that first setback, we refined the concept and entered OU Startup, winning First Prize at the university level. That achievement took us to the national finals of SV_STARTUP VIII in Hanoi, where we competed against teams nationwide and won First Prize.

Along the way, EcoBack pivoted multiple times. Initially, we planned a plugin integrated into e-commerce marketplaces, but realized this made accessing data and controlling platform operations too difficult. We shifted to an independent platform grounded in Vietnam's Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) regulation, connecting manufacturers, consumers, and collection points.

On the technical side, I directly built the web application — handling both frontend and backend. I implemented QR code scanning and user reward point accumulation, used MongoDB Atlas as the database with AI assistance, and deployed the app to a live environment, while collaborating with teammates on the business model and product direction.

03

Looking Back at My Answers

At the university round, a judge asked: 'If Shopee or Tiki builds a similar solution themselves, how will EcoBack compete?' At the time, I answered with first-mover advantage. Looking back, I realize we did not yet own defensible technology — partnering with e-commerce platforms would have been far more realistic than treating them as direct competitors.

At the national finals, mentors noted the idea had potential, but the scope was too broad and we had not worked directly with key stakeholders like collection points or businesses obligated to fund packaging recovery. When asked if we would continue developing the project, I said yes — yet I began to question whether we truly had the resources to turn that commitment into reality.

04

A Difficult Decision

About four months later, we returned to the project in UEH's incubator program. This time, we conducted market research and re-evaluated feasibility. We found existing organizations already operating with a similar direction, and looking honestly at ourselves — all still students — our experience, execution capacity, and manpower were not yet enough to build a product at the scale we envisioned.

In the end, we decided to pause EcoBack. It was not the ending we hoped for at the beginning, but it was a necessary decision after re-examining the market and our execution capacity. EcoBack gave me unforgettable awards, and more importantly, taught me the gap between a good idea and a working model — and that knowing when to pause is just as important as knowing when to push forward.
